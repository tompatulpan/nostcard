How this app works

NostCard shares contact cards that are encrypted in your browser and published to the Nostr network. There are no accounts and no central server — only you hold the keys.

Getting started

  • **Create my card** generates a fresh Nostr identity and encryption key inside your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere yet.
  • Fill in your details and press **Save & publish** — the card is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and published to public Nostr relays.
  • Cards are **live**: saving again updates the same card, and every shared link always shows your latest details.
  • Your keys are stored only in this browser. Use **Advanced → Backup** to move to another device.

Ways to share a card

  • **Share link** — the decryption key is in the #fragment of the URL. Anyone with the full link can view the card in any browser — no app, no account.
  • **Compact link** — the same card as naddr + key with no web address. It cannot be opened in a browser directly — paste it under Contacts instead (or use **Add by link** on the front page). If you also received a web address, you can ignore it — the app never needs it; the card address and key are what matter.
  • **QR code** — the share dialog shows the link as a scannable QR code.
  • **Add-to-contacts link** — opening it downloads the card as a .vcf file straight into the contacts app.
  • **In-person pairing** — exchange cards with someone standing next to you (see below).
  • **Paste a link** — under Contacts, paste any share link to add it as a contact. The card is fetched inside the app, so the link is never opened as a page and never lands in the browser's history.

Use as an app on your phone

  • **iPhone/iPad (Safari):** tap the Share button and choose **Add to Home Screen**. The icon opens the app full-screen, without browser controls. Links you open from other apps (Messages, Mail) open in Safari as normal — they are separate from the home-screen app. To move a card you opened in Safari into the app, use the **Full link** box on the card and paste it under Contacts.
  • **Android (Chrome):** open the menu and choose **Add to Home screen / Install app**. It installs as a standalone app; card links opened inside it do not appear in the browser's address-bar history.
  • **Privacy note:** on a public computer the app clears the #key from the address bar and history immediately and saves nothing. On your own device the key stays in the address bar so the card can be bookmarked. The app can never reach what the browser recorded before it opened (typed URLs, synced history) or the app the link was sent in — for maximum privacy, open links in the installed app or in a private window. On a computer you can remove an old address-bar suggestion by highlighting it and pressing Shift+Delete (desktop Chrome, Edge and Firefox).

The technology — Nostr

Nostr is a decentralized messaging protocol. Your card is a signed event (kind 36350) published to public relays — simple servers that anyone can run. This website is just a static interface; the card data lives on the relays.

  • **Encryption:** AES-256-GCM via the browser's built-in Web Crypto API. The key never leaves your browser except inside the link's #fragment — which browsers never send to any server.
  • **Relays:** they store only ciphertext. A relay operator can see when the card was updated — never the card name or your contact details. Cards published with older app versions may still carry the name until they are re-saved.
  • **No lock-in:** because the data lives on the relays and the key in the link, a card can be opened from any deployment of this app — even one hosted on localhost.
  • **Deletion is best-effort:** deleting a card sends a NIP-09 deletion request. Well-behaved relays comply, but an archived relay may keep a copy — this is a property of all public relays.

Trust models

When someone opens a card link, the app first asks where they are. The answer controls what is stored on that device.

  • **My personal device** — the link is saved locally for quick access and the device is remembered for 30 days. Bookmarking is encouraged and downloading the contact as a .vcf is allowed.
  • **Shared or public computer** — nothing is saved. The session auto-clears when the tab is hidden, after 5 minutes, or on "Done". Downloading is disabled, the page's URL is scrubbed from the address bar and history, and the app restores the only setting it can write here (the language). The browser's own records (typed URLs, synced history) are beyond any web page — for those, use a private/incognito window. Treat it as a danger zone: assume anything on the screen can be recorded — keyloggers and screen capture are beyond any web app.
  • **The link is the password** — anyone holding the full link can read the card. For extra safety, send the base link and the #key part through two different channels (e.g. email + Signal).

In-person pairing (Connect)

**+ Connect** starts a mutual exchange: you get a QR code, the other person scans it, and you each pick which of your cards to share. Both cards end up saved under Contacts. The handshake is an encrypted exchange over the relays and the code stops working after 30 minutes — only accept when the person is actually in front of you. Treat the code like a password: if someone may have photographed it, rotate your card key afterwards to be safe.

Controlling your data

  • **Rotate key** — generates a new encryption key and re-publishes the card. All previous share links stop working immediately.
  • **Delete card** — removes the card from this device and sends a deletion request to all relays (best-effort).
  • **Verify privacy** — the editor's privacy proof shows exactly what a relay can see versus what the key unlocks.

Backup and sync

  • **Backup** exports a JSON file containing everything, including your private keys. Anyone with that file controls your cards — store it securely.
  • **Sync** publishes an encrypted snapshot of your cards to your relays using a generated passphrase. Enter the same passphrase on another device to pull it in — anyone with the passphrase can read and overwrite the snapshot, so keep it secret.
